## Architecture and behavior

- `server/navigation/geometry.ts`: shared per-simulation graph extracted from actual solid fixture polygons, including transformed/rotating bodies. Inactive collapsed surfaces are omitted. Reuse static adjacency; inspect geometry no more than every six ticks (10 Hz). Directed connections use ballistic estimates of normal/charged jumps, drops and grapple-assisted climbs. No full-physics search per bot.
- `server/navigation/navigator.ts`: destination exploration at 5 Hz, local plans at at most 10 Hz, held-action edges at the existing 60 Hz. Unvisited platforms are favored with seeded variation. B1 intentionally does not choose chase, evasion or rescue tactics.
- Launch positions account for the full collision silhouette, including wider rock/spool undersides. An approach side is retained rather than oscillating when the closest edge changes. Air steering clears a ledge before crossing it.
- Ordinary jumps use short press/release; higher routes use a calculated charge. Spring launches are observed through the existing body state, not generated by AI-only impulses.
- Terrain/frog grapple candidates are tested using the eight legal aim directions and actual first-hit raycasts. The ordinary tongue simulation determines the actual hit and moving local anchor. Bots hold/reel, steer laterally around undersides to build swing, and release based on predicted landing, approach to the anchor or maximum hold duration. Moving frogs can be grappled when they are useful on the current route; no combat targeting strategy yet.
- Repeated misses temporarily suppress an aim. Failed edges have a bounded cooldown. Lack of movement or sustained lack of destination progress causes release, repositioning and replanning. No teleport, velocity assignment, force, direct tongue fire or score/state manipulation by the controller.
- Only `server/cpu.ts` and one call in `server/outbreak-room.ts` adapt existing production systems. CPU voting and Bot A lifecycle behavior are unchanged. Countdown, frozen and trapped states interrupt the action plan and produce neutral input. Pauses preserve simulation time.

## Reproduction and evidence

From `frog-network-spike`:

```sh
npm ci
npm test
npm run build
node --import tsx scripts/bot-b1-bench.ts
node --import tsx scripts/bot-b1-network.ts
```

- Full suite: **276 passing tests**, including preservation hashes for baseline sources.
- Small real-physics course: **25/25** runs across five seeds. Running, normal jump, charged jump, gap crossing, grapple/reel/swing/release. All finished within 2.54 s.
- Four fixed destinations per arena × five seeds, 30-second timeout: **72/80** successes (90%); successful median **6.4 s**. Sunny Pond 20/20; Bubblewash 16/20; Canopy 20/20; Toyshop 16/20.
- Success means the frog center enters a 0.85 m destination radius. It does not guarantee a retained landing; spring routes can be fly-throughs. This deliberately includes harder upper routes, not just nearby destinations.
- All 20 modifiers × four arenas × eight controllers: **80** four-second finite-state smoke cases. This verifies safety, not full modifier-aware navigation competence.
- Focused tests cover moving-frog attachment, actual rope contraction, useful release momentum, missed-shot recovery, impossible-goal recovery, moving polygon graph invalidation and input-only operation.
- Local warmed Node/tsx benchmark: worst eight-controller p99 **0.105 ms**; controller + physics p99 **0.292 ms**, against a 16.67 ms tick budget. This is not Render capacity or mobile performance certification. Real-time compiled-server seven-CPU/one-human timings are in the separate network report.
- Independent real WebSocket clients test admission/replacement, all three modes, votes/KEEP, complete matches/rematches, mixed clients, pause/reconnect, human-only host transfer and eight-frog sessions on all four arenas.
- Reports: `results/bot-b1-navigation.json`, `results/bot-b1-network.json`.

## Limitations / playtest focus

- The upper Bubblewash bucket route missed the destination in four of five seeded trials; the high Toyshop spool route missed in four of five. Recovery keeps attempting alternate maneuvers, but these are not reliable routes yet.
- Connection feasibility is a cheap ballistic estimate. It cannot fully predict every intervening silhouette, frog collision or tongue swing. Bots can overshoot or take inefficient routes.
- No advanced tagger pursuit, runner evasion, Freeze rescue priorities or difficulty levels. Purposeful exploration is the B1 goal.
- Chaos is crash-tested, not fully understood by navigation. Sticky wall/ceiling travel, Pogo, swaps and dynamic platform combinations may reduce route reliability. That work remains Bot C.
- Human controls, touch/pointer ownership, viewport sizing, physics, modifiers, art and audio are byte-preserved. No new physical iPhone or Android test is claimed.

## Manual acceptance

1. Create a room; add one CPU manually. Disable Chaos initially to evaluate normal movement. Start and watch destinations, normal/charged jumps, grapples and recovery after falls.
2. Repeat on all four arenas. Watch upper Bubblewash and Toyshop routes especially. A CPU should not hold a useless tongue or run into a wall indefinitely.
3. Add seven CPUs. Check useful movement remains readable, collisions remain ordinary and lobby seats/cosmetics stay unchanged.
4. Try Poison, Freeze and Classic modes; verify roles, scoring, results, CPU votes/KEEP and rematch lifecycle. Strategy will still be basic.
5. Join with another human in a full CPU lobby; verify oldest CPU replacement. Disconnect/reconnect; verify reservation, pause when last human leaves and restoration.
6. Turn Chaos back on for safety checks. Report arena, modifier combination, CPU identity and approximate time for sustained loops or missed releases.
7. Check ordinary desktop and two-thumb mobile play. B1 should not change either input experience.
